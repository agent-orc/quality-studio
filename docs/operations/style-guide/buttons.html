Buttons and add affordances

Button hierarchy follows decision weight. Geometry and alignment stay stable; color and fill communicate emphasis without turning every action into a focal point.

Overview Buttons Forms Notices and layout Applied surfaces

Primary

Use one primary button for the action that commits the current decision, such as Save changes or Onboard repository. Use an action verb and keep it on the trailing edge.

Do
CancelSave changes

One filled action closes the decision path.

Do not
ArchiveSave changes

Two primary buttons make destructive and constructive actions compete.

Secondary

Use a neutral filled or bordered secondary button for a nearby alternative. It must remain legible and visibly interactive in both themes.

Do
CancelSave

Neutral fill keeps Cancel available without competing with Save.

Do not
Cancel nowSave

Accent treatment on the secondary action weakens hierarchy.

Ghost

Use a ghost button for a quiet local action that still needs a reliable hit target. Its boundary is solid when the surrounding surface does not otherwise define the target.

Do
Configure

A solid hairline and stable padding make the action read as intentional.

Do not
configure maybe

Unbounded text with weak contrast does not provide a dependable control.

Add affordances

An additive action is a ghost or secondary button with one aligned leading plus. The plus is part of the label group, not a separate badge or decoration.

Do
+ Add repository

Solid boundary, centered glyph, shared baseline, and one clear label.

Do not · QS-80 before
+ Add repository

Dashed framing and an off-register stray box make a normal action look broken.

Interaction states

StateRequirement
HoverChange background or border through theme tokens. Do not move or resize the button.
FocusShow an accent focus outline with sufficient contrast. Keep the action in normal document order.
DisabledPreserve the label, reduce emphasis, remove the pointer cursor, and keep the reason available in nearby form state.
BusyKeep width stable and change the verb to a compact progress form, for example “Saving…”.
Button patterns are the control-level application of ADM-04, ADM-06, ADM-08, and ADM-09.
